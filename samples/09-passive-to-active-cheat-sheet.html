Turning passive into active

In ordinary prose, passive voice is a matter of taste. In a procedure it decides whether the reader acts or waits. Take the service is restarted after the configuration is changed — does the technician restart it, or does the installer do it for them? One reading gets the job done. The other leaves somebody watching a terminal for something that is never going to happen on its own. Every unnamed actor is a decision you have quietly handed to a reader who knows less about the system than you do.

Three ways to catch one

Add “by zombies”

If you can drop it in after the verb and the sentence still works, it is passive. The file was deleted by zombies. Silly, instant, never wrong.

Look for a to-be verb with an -ed word behind it

is · are · was · were · be · been · being followed by modified · sent · required · configured. Grep for it. That pattern is the whole tell.

Ask who is holding the wrench

Read the sentence and try to picture somebody doing the work. If no one shows up, the sentence is hiding them — and you have found something more interesting than a style problem.

The rotation — the only move you really need

Swap the ends. Drop the “by”. Strip the to-be verb off the front of the action. Three mechanical operations — no judgement required.

Five moves for when the rotation will not go

1
No actor anywhere? Then you have found a real gap.

The sentence is not vague because of grammar. It is vague because nobody knows who does this. Go ask. That question is worth more than the edit.

✗ The certificate is rotated every 90 days.
✓ The provisioning service rotates the certificate every 90 days.

2
In a procedure, the actor is the reader — so delete them.

You do not need a subject at all. The imperative already has one and it is the person holding the page.

✗ The service must be restarted by the administrator.
✓ Restart the service.

3
Dig the verb out of the noun first.

Passive loves company. Half the time the real verb has been mashed into a noun and propped up by a weak one. Free the verb and the passive often falls off by itself.

✗ An inspection of the log is performed by the operator.
✓ The operator inspects the log.

4
If the actor is a system, say so — that is not jargon.

Writers go passive to avoid naming a machine as the doer, as though it were impolite. Machines do things. Let them.

✗ The request is rejected if the token has expired.
✓ The gateway rejects the request if the token has expired.

5
Rebuild the whole sentence rather than fight it.

Some sentences are so tangled that rotating them makes a worse sentence. Do not negotiate. Work out what it is trying to say, look away from the page, and say that.

✗ It was determined that a reboot would be required following the changes that had been made.
✓ Reboot the server after you change the settings.

Leave it alone when

  • Nobody knows who did it. The record was deleted in March. If that is genuinely all you know, saying it plainly beats inventing a culprit.
  • The actor does not matter. The port is reserved for diagnostics. Reserved by whom? Nobody cares, and the sentence is about the port.
  • The thing acted upon is the subject of the paragraph. Keep it in the subject slot so the paragraph does not lurch.
  • You are being deliberately careful. Incident reports and legal writing sometimes need to state what happened without asserting who caused it. That is a decision, not an accident.

The point

A rule you follow because someone told you to is a rule you will apply in the wrong place. Chase the actor, not the construction — and when you cannot find one, you have not found a grammar problem. You have found something nobody knows yet, and that is the most useful thing you will discover all day.
